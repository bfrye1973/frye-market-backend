## 1. Pattern, terminal role and confirmation (125-128)

The chapter presents ending diagonals as terminal motive structures whose completion commonly precedes a fast reversal. It warns that novices identify them too freely. The setup therefore needs more than a narrowing wedge: authorized terminal position, five internal legs and corrective subdivision evidence matter. Source125 places it in fifth-wave position of an impulse and C position of ABC; the review on153 specifies C of flats/zigzags. Do not extend those permissions to arbitrary A, B, triangleE or ordinary midtrend waves without separate authority.

For the contracting form discussed, motive-direction legs shrink: wave3 is shorter than1 and5 shorter than3. The source sometimes calls those internal legs “impulse” waves in conversational prose; the Euro close-up146 shows that their actual internals are single/multiple zigzags. Keep outer directional function separate from an orthodox impulse subtype. Ordinary impulse rules cannot be applied indiscriminately to diagonal overlap.

The three source approaches are distinct choices: anticipate as5 develops with a length-based protection ceiling; wait for a decisive break of the2-4 line; or wait for the wave4 extreme to break. The author also demonstrates a fourth, modified lower-degree trigger in TCK. These are evidence stages, not mandatory Engine16 order instructions. “Decisive” lacks a numeric close/buffer policy and must be specified before coding. Target at the origin is a guideline; it does not structurally force a future print.
